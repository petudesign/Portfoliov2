Helsinki Sports Map

Finding somewhere to play a sport in Helsinki meant several searches, a visit to each venue’s website and then another app for the route. I built one map for finding a venue and getting there.

I designed the discovery flow, built it and its data integrations with coding agents, and review venue data myself before it’s shown as fact.

Bring the venue and the journey together

Problem

Finding a sport meant scattered search results, incomplete information and more searching on venue websites, then planning the trip somewhere else.

What I did

Sport filters, address search, optional current location and venue details lead straight into walking, cycling, driving and HSL routes. A linked text list works with a keyboard and screen reader, so venues stay browsable without tapping markers.

Mobile first

I expect people to use the map while choosing a destination or already moving around Helsinki, so the phone came first.

Desktop

The list first opened as a drawer over the map on every screen. On desktop it became a fixed column beside the map, so browsing and seeing where venues are happen at once; phones keep the drawer.

Rework the interface, then the data

What changed

The interface was rebuilt around one panel: search, Near me and sport filters at the top, places below, and a venue opening in the same panel with directions. Near me sorts places by distance. When prices or opening hours are missing, the card says so and links to the official page.

Still open

It’s much easier to use now, but there are still rough spots I want to fix. The bigger gap is the data: accessibility and prices are missing for many venues, and that’s what I want to improve next.

Replace the custom map to focus on the product

Problem

I first built a custom renderer for a distinctive look. As the dataset grew, smooth zooming and rendering became a project of its own.

What helped, for a while

Spatial chunks, rendering in a background worker and reused frames helped, but optimisation kept taking time away from the product.

Decision

I switched to MapLibre GL JS with OpenFreeMap’s Positron basemap, with its touch gestures, clustering and 2D or tilted views.

Trade-off

The switch gave up some character in exchange for the whole city, smooth zooming and time for helping people find a place and get there.

Use the right source for each part of the journey

Routes

Walking, cycling and driving routes sit alongside public transport from HSL’s Digitransit routing API, with fare zones and ticket reminders.

Venues

Venue data comes from LIPAS, enriched with Helsinki Service Map information. Coverage is citywide, but completeness varies by field.

Addresses

Helsinki’s Paikkatietohaku provides address lookup through separately requested API access, and the city’s street index supports suggestions.

Prices

Free use can be confirmed from the data. Paid access and exact prices need checking, which is what the review queue is for.

Review discrepancies before treating them as facts

Problem

People should be able to make a practical decision from what the map shows. A missing price can’t be shown as free access.

What I did

A manual review queue keeps incoming venue data apart from what I’m prepared to approve. I compare it with the official source, accept, correct or reject it, and export approved records. Approval never publishes anything by itself.

Evidence, not probability

Each candidate gets a rule-based evidence score: a recognised price format, a match to the LIPAS venue and a fresh source add points, while an unfinished cross-check holds points back.

Next

Price conditions matter too: an adult ticket, a concession and an equipment charge are different costs. Accessibility details come next, so people can judge a venue before travelling.

How I worked with agents

Split

Agents mapped the open data sources and their terms, LIPAS, Helsinki Service Map and Digitransit, and built the integrations. I designed the flow for a visitor new to Helsinki, decided when to drop the custom map and review venue data myself.

Order of work

The interface still needs polish. I wanted the whole journey, from finding a venue to getting there, to work as intended before refining the details.

Make Helsinki the first city, with room for others

Built to expand

City-specific configuration and data providers are separate from the interface, so another city means adapting sources rather than rebuilding the app.

Visitors

A Chinese-speaking friend has helped with translations towards Chinese support for visitors. A trending layer would need enough real activity first; the current preview is illustrative.
