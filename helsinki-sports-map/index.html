Helsinki Sports Map

A map for finding sports venues in Helsinki, with sport filters, venue details and route planning.

It started with a small area around the Olympic Stadium. I expanded it with public venue data; Helsinki is still the first city, and its coverage is being completed.

The problem

Finding a sports venue means checking several separate sources.

Locations, sports offered, prices, accessibility and transit information are fragmented. HSM brings those details into one map.

What I built

A bilingual venue map with search, filters and routes.

  • MapLibre map with 2D and tilted views, plus grouped venue markers.
  • Sport filters, address search, optional location, venue details and routing.
  • A reusable data pipeline and custom API combining LIPAS with Helsinki's official service information.
  • A keyboard-operated venue list that stays in sync with the map and selected place.
Problems and responses

Large datasets slowed rendering on zoom.

Problem: Geographic data made the map slower as the view changed.

Response: Split data into spatial chunks, moved background drawing to a worker, and reused the previous render during zoom.

A synthetic headless Edge benchmark showed median background-draw time falling from about 28–58 ms to 3–4 ms. This measures drawing work, not real-device frame rate.

Venue data has missing and unclear fields.

Problem: Sport categories, prices and usage rights can be incomplete or ambiguous.

Response: Normalize details against their sources and mark unknown information as missing or needing verification instead of guessing.

Map-only interaction excludes some users.

Problem: Small markers and a visual canvas make keyboard and screen-reader access harder.

Response: Add a keyboard-operated venue list linked to the map and venue details.

Keyboard access is still incomplete. Manual NVDA, VoiceOver and TalkBack testing is outstanding, and an accessibility issue in the fallback map remains open.

Where it stands

Helsinki is the only city in the current version.

The product includes search, filters, venue details, routing, 2D and tilted map views, and Finnish and English interfaces. Venue coverage is still being expanded. User research and product impact have not yet been validated.

What comes next

Complete coverage, verify the data and test real tasks.

Next: complete Helsinki venue coverage, enrich prices, accessibility and booking information from reliable sources, and test keyboard and screen-reader access, including the fallback map. Multi-city support and travel-time or midpoint search remain future explorations.

The question

How might a city-scale map make local sports opportunities easier to discover and compare?

My contribution

I am working across product framing, information structure, interface direction and the relationship between public data and user-facing discovery.

What is still open

The next decisions concern coverage, data quality, filtering and how much context a person needs before choosing a place or activity.

From places to decisions

The current interface pairs a clustered map with a searchable facility list, so people can explore the same venue data spatially or by scanning results. The map is useful only if it helps answer a real question: what can I do, where, and what should I look at next?
