Near-expiry checks in grocery stores rely on manual work and memory, and food goes to waste when a shelf is missed. Drawing on four years on the shop floor, I designed a service concept to make that work easier and the discounted food easier to find.

It connects an employee tool, an in-store point and clearer information in the app. The prototype is interactive; the store pilot hasn’t been carried out.

Start from the work I knew

Problem

In more than four years in grocery retail, I saw expiry checks rely on manual inspection, memory, shelf familiarity and local routines.

Focus

Discounted products already sell well in many stores. I focused on the work that gets them to customers: finding them, handling them consistently and making the remaining selection visible.

The frustration

The same shelves could need checking again later, and keeping track of what was done was hard when the store was busy.

Make the shelf the organising structure

What I did

The employee tool is a map of the physical shelf. Each square is one shelf section, and its colour shows whether it needs attention, so the next employee sees what’s already done.

Where it runs

On the handheld scanners or internal tools employees already use. How expiry data reaches it would still need solving; inventory integration is outside this prototype.

The workflow

Check the physical products, decide how to handle them and update the status for the next shift. A department view moves between shelf units.

My scope decision: use the first prototype to explore one repeated shelf-checking task and its handover, before any custom hardware or deeper app work.

Make the in-store point easy to recognise

Idea

A consistent cooler, shelf or display near checkout brings near-expiry products together. The message is practical: useful food, a clear discount and a changing selection.

Start small

I explored a branded installation, but the first pilot should use simple signage, consistent labels and an existing product zone, to learn whether placement alone helps customers notice and choose the products.

Match the digital promise to store reality

Problem

Near-expiry stock changes fast: an item may sell or sit in someone’s basket before the app updates.

Decision

The S-kaupat section would show broad signals about nearby stores and categories. I scoped out reservations and exact future availability.

Honest signals

A signal like “available today” disappears when the selection is too small or uncertain. The app extension depends on a reliable in-store process and stays secondary to it.

Define what a useful first pilot would show

Plan

One store, one or two product categories, one to two weeks, to test the employee workflow and the in-store point before investing in a wider system.

Measures

  • Employee workflow: time, missed items, repeated checks
  • Customers: noticeability and pickup
  • Operations: recovered value, unsold items

These are planned measures, not results. Custom hardware, deeper integrations and personalised alerts would need evidence from that first step.
