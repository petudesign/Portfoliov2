Peluutin

Junior coaches had no easy way to keep track of each player’s minutes. I built Peluutin with two football coaches: playing time and substitutions on a phone, and training exercises that replay on any phone.

I designed the product and built it with coding agents. Every feature started from a coach’s request or a testing session.

Start with every player’s minutes

Problem

Two junior coaches needed a record of each player’s minutes that they could review themselves and explain to parents.

What I did

Playing-time tracking came first. Every player’s minutes sit on their shirt, with the bench, score and match clock in the same view. Football and futsal each have their own pitch, formations and timing.

A competing app

One coach had struggled with another app. Trying it myself, I found inconsistent interactions and too many menus, so I kept match actions close together and cut the navigation needed during a game.

Who needs minutes

Each player has a small indicator. Two wavy lines mean they are close to the average of the active players; an arrow up or down shows they are above or below it, so the coach sees who needs more minutes without doing the maths.

Before Peluutin, we didn’t measure playing time. Doing it by hand would have been too hard, so I glanced at the clock and tried to remember how long each player had been on.

Keep the substitution beside the bench

Problem

On a phone, the pitch and the bench don’t fit in one view, so a substitution meant jumping between them mid-game.

What I did

Choosing a substitute opens a compact grid of on-field players right below the bench, so the coach picks who comes off without going back to the pitch.

From a request

A coach wanted lineups ready before kick-off. Saved groups now let a whole group change at once, and several players can be selected on the pitch.

After the match

The statistics page shows each player’s all-time minutes, with goals, scorers and match history. Excel export gives coaches something to share with parents.

Substitutions stay under control, and playing time really evens out over longer stretches. One match is easy to manage, but in tournaments Peluutin has helped.

Make the score the button

Problem

During a match the coach looks at the phone for a second at a time. The score and the clock were small, and each score had its own − and + buttons, so marking a goal meant hitting a small target.

What changed

The score is now the button. A tap adds a goal and asks who scored, and a long press opens a sheet for taking one back. The score and the match clock are bigger, and the sport switch moved to team settings to make room.

A second request: exercises you can replay

Problem

The coaches also needed to plan training. The Finnish Football Association planner my girlfriend used wouldn’t run on her newer Mac, and it only made static exercises.

What I did

A football planner with 2D and 3D views and a timeline that sets when each pass and run happens and how long it takes. Exercises are made on a tablet or computer and replay on any phone.

Why 3D

Some movements are easier to read in depth, the boards around the pitch leave room for things like club sponsors, and, honestly, I think it simply looks better.

In use

Instead of writing a long message about what happens and in what order, a coach sends a link. The coaches have already planned real training sessions with it.

The association’s planner only made static exercises. With replays and share links, showing what we’re going to do takes much less effort.

One iteration shaped by tablet feedback

Problem

I had designed editing for a computer: Ctrl+Z, copy and paste, area selection and the Delete key. On a tablet, one tester found removing things from the pitch too frustrating.

What I did

A Layers panel lists every player, piece of equipment and route with its own delete button, and Undo sits in the bottom bar instead of behind a shortcut.

Smaller frictions

Placing several cones meant reopening the menu each time, so placement menus now stay open, and thin lines got larger hit areas. The session also exposed a bug: an action after a run started from the player’s old position, so the replay didn’t match the plan.

Her way of planning

This tester plans in light mode, in 2D, on a plain grass pitch, so each of those is an option. She is left-handed and found the tools more natural on the left, so the tool rail can switch sides.

Let recipients open the exercise directly

Sharing

A PNG or a read-only playable link opens straight to the exercise on a phone, without creating a team or adding players. Names and numbers are left out by default, and the coach’s original stays unchanged.

Storage

Team data stays in the browser, with file export for backups. Sign-in waits until I know how to store children’s personal data safely: how player information is stored, accessed and deleted.

How I worked with agents

Split

A coding agent built the first versions from my prompts. Agents get me 60–70% of the way; I designed the flows from the coaches’ requests, directed the interaction details and reviewed every change on a local server.

Next

Basketball and floorball come next. Each needs its own match workflow, because substitution rules and timing change how a coach runs a game. Opt-in analytics and more coaches will show which flows need work.
