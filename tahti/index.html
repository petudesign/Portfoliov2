Tahti

A reading experiment about pace, focus and the friction of starting dense text.

Tahti presents selected text one word at a time at a controlled rhythm. The goal is not to prove that this is a better way to read everything, but to test where pacing might help.

Problem

Dense text can be difficult to start and stay with.

Tahti tests whether a controlled one-word rhythm can reduce visual load without pretending to replace normal reading.

My contribution

Product framing, interaction design and working extension.

I shaped the MVP, built the reader flow and tuned pacing, controls and comfort settings around the experiment.

Current state

A working experiment that still needs evidence.

The next step is testing real reading tasks to understand where the format helps and where it damages comprehension.

Tahtinoun · pace, rhythm

Who it is for

People who struggle to start or stay with dense text when the barrier is visual load, focus or reading fatigue rather than lack of interest.

Why an extension

The first useful test needs one core behaviour: select text on a web page and read it in a focused overlay, without accounts or full-page parsing.

What success means

The user starts faster, stays with the text longer and still understands enough for the pace trade-off to be worthwhile.

A careful claim

Tahti should not be framed as a speed-reading promise. Reading one word at a time can remove useful context, make backtracking harder and flatten the natural rhythm of paragraphs.

A more honest direction is reading support: pace, focus and temporary structure when ordinary reading feels demanding.

Product principle: Help users control reading pace without pretending that faster reading is automatically better reading.
