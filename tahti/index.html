Tahti

Long, dense text can feel heavy before I’ve read a word. Tahti is a Chrome extension that shows selected text one word at a time, at a pace that starts slow and settles to the one you choose.

I built it from my own reading friction. It’s a working experiment, and how well it works for other readers is still untested.

My role
Product, UX and interaction design
Timeline
Jun 2026
Format
Chrome extension

Begin with my own reading friction

Problem

Mild astigmatism and ADD make sustained reading hard for me, and a long text can feel heavy before I start.

Idea

Show one word at a time, to cut the visual load and give a rhythm to follow.

What it proves

In my own use it has helped in some situations and let me move through text faster. That shaped the experiment, but it doesn’t prove a benefit for other readers.

Keep the first version narrow

Decision

Start with text the reader selects, launched from the right-click menu and read in an overlay on the current page, so the reader stays in control.

Left out

Automatic article extraction, PDFs, accounts, analytics and AI features. Headings, images, tables and code would each need their own handling.

One step less

The first version had to be opened from the toolbar after selecting text. A right-click launch removed that step.

Make pace and controls easier to follow

What I did

The word, progress and controls sit in one central stack, close to where the eye already is.

Pacing

Reading starts slower, then approaches the chosen pace, with extra time for punctuation and longer words. Pause and ten-word jumps let the reader step out of the stream.

Judge understanding as well as comfort

Risk

A calmer presentation can still lose sentence structure, scanning back and the wider context.

Next

Reading tasks with other readers would compare how easy it is to start, focus over time, fatigue and comprehension, before expanding the format.
