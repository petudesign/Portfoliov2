Tahti 2025

A controlled reading rhythm for dense text.

A Chrome extension that presents selected text one word at a time, exploring pace, focus and the friction of starting to read.

My role
Product, UX & interaction design
Problem

Starting dense text can feel demanding.

My own reading difficulties led me to explore whether showing less text at once could make reading easier to begin and sustain.

My contribution

Build and refine a focused reading mode.

I scoped and built the selected-text reader, then refined its launch, pacing and controls. My own use informed the iterations; testing with other readers is still ahead.

Current state

A working reading experiment.

The extension reads selected text one word at a time. My own experience is encouraging in some situations; comprehension and usefulness for others remain untested.

Keep the first version narrow

Selecting text and launching from the right-click menu keeps the reader on the current page. Automatic article extraction, PDFs and accounts are outside the MVP.

Make pace and controls easier to follow

The word, progress and controls sit in one central stack. Reading starts slower, then approaches the chosen pace, with extra time for punctuation and longer words. Pause and ten-word navigation let the reader interrupt the stream.

Judge understanding as well as comfort

A calmer presentation can still lose sentence structure, scanning and context. Planned reading tasks would compare ease of starting, sustained focus, fatigue and comprehension before expanding the format.

Begin with my own reading friction

Mild astigmatism and ADD can make sustained reading difficult for me, and I am not a particularly fast line-by-line reader. A long text can feel heavy before I start. Tahti explores whether showing one word at a time can reduce that initial visual load and provide a rhythm to follow.

In my own use, it has helped in some situations and let me move through text faster. That personal experience shaped the experiment, but it does not establish a benefit for other readers.

Choose selected text as the first unit

I built a framework-free Chrome extension that reads a selected passage in an overlay on the current page. Keeping the selection with the user limited the implementation and made it possible to explore the reading experience directly.

My scope decision: start with text the reader selects. That keeps control with the user and lets me explore the reading interaction before solving full article extraction.

Headings, images, tables, embeds and code would need their own handling. I left automatic extraction, PDFs, accounts, analytics and AI features outside the MVP.

  1. Select text
  2. Right-click
  3. Read with Tahti
  4. Set pace
  5. Read, pause or rewind
  6. Return to page

Reduce the effort around the reading word

The first version required opening the extension after selecting text. I added a right-click launch to remove that step. Within the overlay, the early layout spread settings and controls across several areas; I brought the word, progress and controls into a central stack to keep them close to the reader’s focus.

I also replaced WPM and font-size steppers with compact sliders to make the settings less visually dominant. Font size, dark/light mode and a short countdown provide basic comfort controls.

Give the stream a readable rhythm

A uniform words-per-minute interval made every word advance at the same rate. I added extra time for sentence endings, softer punctuation breaks and longer words. The first words start slower and build toward the chosen pace.

Play, pause, reset and ten-word backward or forward controls let the reader stop or revisit a passage. The purpose of these adjustments is to make the pace manageable; their effect on comprehension still needs testing.

Evaluate comprehension before expanding

The format is related to Rapid Serial Visual Presentation (RSVP), where words appear sequentially in one position. The research provides context for the experiment, but does not validate Tahti itself. One-word presentation removes paragraph shape, easy scanning and surrounding words that a reader may need.

Research references
  • Adaptive RSVP on mobile devices
  • Spritz and RSVP reading comprehension
  • RSVP and dynamic text for visually impaired readers
  • Perceptual and cognitive limits in RSVP reading

My next step is to use real articles, notes and documentation, then test with other readers. I want to assess ease of starting, sustained focus, fatigue and understanding, including which text types are unsuitable for this mode.

The next expansion depends on that evidence. If it helps other readers while preserving understanding, I would explore an article mode with pauses for headings, images and tables. Tahti remains a reading-support experiment; my own positive experience is not a demonstrated general speed or comprehension improvement.
