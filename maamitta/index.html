Maamitta

Quoting a landscaping or paving job in Finland usually means bouncing between four tools: a map app to check property borders, eyeballing how much house footprint to subtract, a spreadsheet for cubic metres, density, waste, pallets, truck loads and 25.5% VAT, and a separate document for the client quote.

Maamitta puts that chain into one browser tool for Finnish landscaping, groundworks, paving, fencing and property maintenance contractors, as well as landowners. Click an official National Land Survey (MML) parcel to cut out the buildings automatically, draw only the part of the yard being paved, or trace a driveway centerline and expand it to 3.5 m or 4.0 m wide. Account for terrain slope, combine multiple work types into one estimate, and export a client-ready PDF, a 4-line WhatsApp summary, or GeoJSON and KML for CAD/GIS.

One chain from parcel to quote

The gap

Existing Finnish map apps like Karttaselain, Mapis and MML Karttapaikka are strong for hiking and checking parcel borders, but they stop at square metres. None turn a yard measurement into truck loads, paver pallets, edging metres, slope corrections and a VAT breakdown ready to send to a client.

Partial yards and driveways

Nobody paves an entire property. Alongside one-click parcel picking, a contractor can draw just the front yard being paved, trace a driveway centerline and expand it into a 3.5 m or 4.0 m wide corridor, and stack multiple work types plus custom labour (such as 4 hours of excavator work) into one quote.

Why a web app before a native iOS app

Contractors often measure on site with a phone and finish the quote on a laptop in the evening. Shipping as a mobile-first web app works on both screens right away, avoids App Store review and the 15–30% fee, and lets beta testers open it without creating an account.

Dead ends and design decisions

Cadastral borders and red-green colourblindness

MML’s official raster property borders looked like thick marker strokes when zoomed in, and lightening them to white made them vanish against bright roofs and forest canopies. I switched to fetching visible parcels as vector features and rendering a dark 3.2 px casing under a crisp cyan dashed line. Every measurement layer follows an Okabe-Ito-inspired palette (blue measured area, amber dashed building cutout, violet linear measurement) so nothing merges under red-green colourblindness.

Automatic building subtraction

OpenStreetMap’s Overpass API was slow in rural areas and missed newer buildings. Moving to MML’s official building register cut lookup times to around 100 ms with full Finnish coverage. Subtraction checks real geometric overlap area (at least 6 m²), so a neighbouring house sharing a wall or border isn’t cut out by mistake while L- and C-shaped footprints stay accurate.

Satellite tilt vs. ground truth

In satellite view, an amber building cutout can look shifted a metre or two off the roof. That is satellite parallax: photos shot at a slight angle make taller roofs lean away from their foundations. The official MML vector polygon is the ground truth, not the tilted roof. Satellite imagery can also lag a year or two behind new construction while the MML register is already current, which is why the map shows the photo’s capture date and resolution.

Archipelago, coastlines and stray clicks

A hand-drawn 40-point Finland mask accidentally sliced off the Turku archipelago and Åland, so I replaced it with an official 382-point maritime boundary. Shoreline plots with more than 24 vertices lost all edge-length labels under a hard limit; replacing it with screen-space collision avoidance kept readable labels on complex plots. Starting in Area mode also dropped stray points while panning, and Hand mode still grabbed corner handles; locking startup to Hand/Pan (H) and adding a 30-step Ctrl+Z geometry history stopped accidental edits.

The Palsta mark

Instead of a map pin or an M-monogram, the mark (Palsta) is a parcel grid with one highlighted plot. It stays readable at 16 px favicon size and renders as a crisp vector in the PDF quote footer.

Scrapped idea: PDF site-plan overlay

I asked the agent to prototype uploading a yard plan or site drawing PDF so the app could trace areas straight from the drawing. The manual version (dragging a semi-transparent PDF over the satellite map, scaling and rotating it by hand, then locking it to trace) turned out to be the wrong tool. Maamitta already pulls official parcel borders and building footprints directly from MML data, so aligning a PDF by eye just added work, and most small residential paving or gravel jobs do not even come with a formal plan to upload. I cut the feature instead of keeping extra clutter.

Breaking the app with agents

Before sharing the link, I ran three adversarial agent reviews in parallel, followed by a senior architecture pass.

  1. Code & Security AuditorProxy · calculators · geometry

    Found a path-traversal risk (.., \) and cached 4xx/5xx error responses in the MML proxy, NaN/Infinity crashes when extreme or negative numbers hit the calculator, and a chaining bug when subtracting multiple buildings.

    What changedStrict path validation, caching only 2xx responses, sanitized calculator inputs, and sequential cutout chaining.

  2. UI Breaker & Chaos TesterExtreme values · PDF · storage

    Multi-zone quotes with more than 7 rows overflowed the first PDF page, corrupted local browser storage could crash startup, and dragging a vertex off the map canvas left the drag state stuck.

    What changedAutomatic PDF pagination, schema-validated storage recovery on load, and window-level pointer release handling.

  3. Live UX & Bilingual QA1440 px & 390 px · FI & EN

    Tested all 9 job types on desktop and phone in Finnish and English, catching a two-line English field label misalignment, overlapping tooltips, and Finnish strings leaking into English mode.

    What changedAligned input label grids, directional tooltips, and complete bilingual coverage across UI and PDF output.

  4. Architecture & PerformanceBundle · drag saves · tests

    Dragging a corner vertex triggered browser storage writes at 60 fps, and all mapping, PDF and GIS export libraries shipped in a single JS bundle.

    What changedDebounced saves (300 ms plus a pagehide flush) and expanded coverage to 23 unit tests. Split the original single JS bundle into cached vendor chunks (React, map libraries) and a small app chunk (~49 kB gzipped), so returning users only re-download the app code after an update. PDF and GIS export code (~124 kB gzipped) loads only when a user actually exports.

What I caught that the agents didn’t

Automated checks and agent reviews caught crashes and edge cases, but missed four things you only notice by using the tool like a contractor:

  • Whole parcel vs. partial yard

    One-click parcel picking works for sodding or mowing a whole yard, not for paving. Nobody paves an entire property, so drawing partial areas and 3.5 m driveway corridors had to be just as quick to start.

  • Topographic map contrast

    The white floating controls read cleanly over satellite imagery, but lost contrast against MML’s bright white topographic map. Adding a stronger border and shadow on floating pills when the topographic layer is active kept them legible.

  • Satellite parallax

    Angled satellite photos made accurate MML building cutouts look slightly shifted on taller roofs, so the map now surfaces the photo’s date and resolution and treats the official vector footprint as the ground truth.

  • Distant sites in one PDF

    When a quote combined two sites far apart (say Turku and Helsinki), a single PDF snapshot zoomed out until neither yard was readable. Distant zones now get separate close-up map views.

Client-ready PDFs and field mobile

Offscreen PDF snapshots

Capturing the visible map canvas produced screenshots at whatever zoom level the user happened to be on, cluttered with drag handles and neighbouring parcel lines. A separate hidden map renderer frames the measured zones cleanly without edit handles or neighbouring parcel clutter, and closes right after capture so the user’s camera never jumps.

Built for outdoor phone use

44×44 px touch targets, 16 px inputs so iOS Safari never zooms when editing quantities, safe-area 100dvh layout, tabular numbers so totals never jump while typing, and a centre crosshair mode for placing corners with one thumb.

Privacy, security and cookie-free analytics

Zero-cost serverless proxy

Because MML’s parcel and building APIs require an API key, hosting on a plain static host like GitHub Pages wasn’t an option without exposing it. All MML requests pass through a Cloudflare Pages serverless proxy with an encrypted secret and edge caching, keeping hosting at €0/month.

No owner data, opt-in beta telemetry

Property owner names are restricted personal data under Finnish KTJ licensing and GDPR, so Maamitta never fetches or stores them; it shows the open parcel ID with a copy button and links to MML’s official portal. Projects stay in the user’s browser, with no account required. For beta testing, an opt-in consent bar enables EU-hosted PostHog session replay (with text inputs like addresses and names automatically masked), error tracking and custom events that log only coarse area brackets.

Where it stands, and what comes next

Current status

Live at maamitta.pages.dev. Tested thoroughly by myself and by agents across desktop and mobile, but not yet field-tested with paying contractors.

Next: contractor testing

Put Maamitta in front of 3–5 Finnish landscaping and groundworks contractors, with no account needed, to see which job types and exports (PDF quote or 4-line WhatsApp summary) save real time and where field use slows them down.

Finding pilot testers

Cold-emailing random construction companies is a waste of time, and paid B2B lead databases did not make sense for a zero-budget beta. To find Finnish paving, landscaping and groundworks contractors who actually quote residential yards, I defined the filtering criteria and used an AI coding agent to build a small Python script on top of the Finnish Patent and Registration Office’s open YTJ API.

The script filters out dormant companies and housing cooperatives by checking active VAT, prepayment and employer register entries. It then scans each company’s homepage and contact page for Maamitta’s specific job types (stone paving, sodding, driveways, drainage, and soil or gravel delivery) and extracts direct phone numbers and emails. The first nationwide pass scanned 59 active companies and produced 46 contactable pilot candidates, 31 of them strong matches, exported to an Excel workbook with live outreach tracking and short beta invites written around each company’s services.

Planned for v1.1+

MML’s 2 m elevation model for more accurate slope and drainage calculations; saved company price presets (unit prices and VAT are already editable per job, so this would persist a contractor’s own rates, hourly labour and margin across quotes) plus lightweight site notes and photos; optional cloud sync only once repeat users ask for phone-to-desktop sync; and a shareable interactive quote link alongside the PDF.
