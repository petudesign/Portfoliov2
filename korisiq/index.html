KorisLab 2026

Explore Finnish basketball through questions, teams and games.

An analysis tool for the men’s and women’s Korisliiga. Search finds teams and players, and can also answer supported questions about a season.

My role
Product design, development & analytics
Try KorisLab ↗
Problem

Basketball statistics need context.

A box score shows one game. KorisLab connects teams, players and seasons so those numbers can be explored and compared in context.

My contribution

Design, development and analysis.

I built KorisLab around comparison and accessible search, connecting statistics with the questions users want to explore.

Current state

Two leagues, with season data from 2024–25 onward.

Both leagues are included, with data mainly from Basket.fi. Not every game has been validated for every tool; coverage and verification are ongoing.

Why I built it

I enjoy basketball, comparison and analytics. A box score did not tell me enough, and I could not find the comparison experience I wanted. KorisLab is my way of bringing more of the analytical depth I associate with the NBA to Finnish basketball, starting at a smaller scale.

Search is the starting point

The search goes beyond team and player lookup: a supported season question can return an answer and link to the relevant profile. The goal is to make KorisLab easy to use without first knowing which view contains the information.

Over time, I want users to ask a much wider range of basketball questions and be taken to the right page, given an answer, or both.

A box score did not tell me enough

I enjoy watching basketball and comparing teams and players. As my interest in analytics grew, I wanted to explore more than the numbers in a single box score. I could not find the comparison experience I was looking for, so I created KorisLab.

I want Finnish basketball to get more attention. The analytical depth I associate with the NBA inspired me to bring some of that approach to Finland, starting at a smaller scale with Korisliiga.

Ask a question and follow the answer

Ease of use is central to what I want KorisLab to become. Search is more than a way to find a player or team: it connects a basketball question with an answer and a useful next step.

For example, asking who had the most assists in the 2025–26 season returns a leader, the total and a link to the player profile. The answer is tied to the selected season and available data.

The current search handles supported questions. My longer-term aim is for users to ask a much wider range of basketball questions in their own words. KorisLab would then open the relevant view, answer directly, or combine both, so users can begin with what they want to know.

Explore a season, then follow the detail

The live product connects a season overview with team and player profiles, match statistics and analysis views. Users can compare shooting profiles, pace and efficiency, then follow links into the underlying team, player or game.

Compare team statistics with a player on and off court

Matchup Lab lets users compare a team's statistics with a selected player on court and on the bench. Side-by-side metrics and shot-profile views make the comparison easier to explore.

Not every game has been validated for every tool yet. In this example, seven team games were excluded because their play-by-play or lineup data did not pass validation. Coverage is shown alongside the results so users can see which sample the comparison represents.

Give the comparison its own context

I want the tools to provide context for basketball statistics, and to make the context needed to interpret the tools visible too. In this example, BC Nokia's point rate looks much lower with Alina Ahonen on court. That state covers only about 2.4 minutes per game, compared with 37.6 minutes on the bench.

The rates are normalized per 40 minutes, so these are not raw point totals. The much smaller on-court sample matters when interpreting the difference. Showing minutes, coverage and calculation notes helps users read the figures in context.

Interpreting the comparison also requires checking when those minutes occurred. Short stints can include “garbage time”, when a game is already effectively decided. This is possible context to investigate, rather than an established explanation for Ahonen's minutes. The roughly twofold difference here does not prove that she caused the team's scoring rate to fall.

Current data and the search for more

KorisLab’s current data comes mainly from Basket.fi statistics. The interface distinguishes verified game statistics from calculated estimates, while coverage and verification remain ongoing.

I have contacted the Finnish Basketball Association by email to explore what additional data might be available in Finland. I am still awaiting a response, so this remains an open inquiry.

Making basketball content discoverable

I added a foundation for search-engine and AI-search discovery:

  • Page-specific metadata and canonical URLs: titles and descriptions identify the content, league and season. Chart filters point to the underlying game's canonical page.
  • A generated sitemap: lists public views, team and player profiles, verified games and published analysis articles for crawlers to find.
  • Pre-rendered content and structured data: player and verified-game pages include HTML summaries with statistics and source links before JavaScript runs. Schema.org ProfilePage and SportsEvent data describe the entities; robots.txt explicitly allows OpenAI's search crawler on public pages.

Deeper analysis for more people around the game

I want KorisLab to become useful for commentators, coaches and players, as well as fans. The aim is to make basketball analysis easier to explore for people with different questions and levels of statistical knowledge.

Longer term, I plan to explore computer vision to collect more detailed game data and support deeper analysis. This is a future direction; it is not part of the current product.
