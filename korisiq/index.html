KorisLab 2026

Explore Finnish basketball through questions, teams and games.

I designed and built an analysis tool for the men’s and women’s Korisliiga, connecting season statistics, player profiles and match analysis. Search lets people begin with a team, a player or a supported question about a season.

My role
Product design, development & analytics
Try KorisLab ↗
Problem

A box score leaves questions unanswered.

I wanted to compare teams and players across games, and understand the context behind their numbers. I could not find the comparison experience I wanted for Finnish basketball.

My contribution

Design, development and analytics.

I designed and built the analysis experience, making questions a route into the data and showing the context needed to interpret comparisons. I guide the coding agent’s implementation and review the working product.

Current state

Two leagues, an evolving product.

KorisLab covers the men’s and women’s Korisliiga from 2024–25 onward, mainly using Basket.fi data. Coverage varies by tool; ongoing work includes interface improvements and data validation.

Begin with a basketball question

Search finds teams and players, and answers supported season questions with a route into the relevant profile. My aim is to let people start with what they want to know, without first learning the navigation.

Build the comparison experience I wanted

I enjoy basketball and comparing teams and players. As my interest in analytics grew, a single box score felt insufficient, and I could not find the comparison experience I wanted. I built KorisLab to connect season overviews, profiles, match statistics and analysis.

I want Finnish basketball to receive more attention. The depth of NBA analysis inspired me to explore a smaller-scale version for Korisliiga, with fans, commentators, coaches and players in mind.

Let a question lead into the data

I want people to begin with a basketball question without having to know which analysis view contains the answer. I made search a starting point for supported season questions alongside team and player lookup. Asking who led the 2025–26 season in assists returns the player, total and a profile link, tied to the selected season and available data.

The answer gives users a starting point and a next step into the underlying detail. I want to expand the range of questions over time, returning an answer, opening a relevant view or combining both.

Give each comparison the context it needs

Matchup Lab compares team statistics with a player on court and on the bench, using side-by-side metrics and shot profiles.

My design priority: make the context needed to read the comparison visible beside the figures. A striking difference needs its sample size, playing time and calculation method.

  • Coverage: this BC Nokia example includes 17 of 24 games. Seven were excluded because play-by-play or lineup data did not pass validation.
  • Playing time: Ahonen’s on-court state averages about 2.4 minutes per covered game, versus 37.6 on the bench.
  • Calculation: the displayed rates are normalised per 40 minutes, so a small sample can produce a large apparent difference.

The roughly twofold scoring-rate difference does not establish that Ahonen caused lower scoring. Short stints can include garbage time, when a game is effectively decided. That is context to investigate, not a confirmed explanation for her minutes. Minutes, coverage and calculation notes help users judge the comparison.

Make the content discoverable beyond the app

I added concrete foundations for search-engine and AI-search discovery:

  • Metadata and canonical URLs: titles and descriptions identify league, season and content; chart filters point to the underlying game.
  • A generated sitemap: covers public views, profiles, verified games and analysis articles.
  • Pre-rendered summaries: player and verified-game HTML includes statistics and source links before JavaScript runs, with Schema.org ProfilePage and SportsEvent data. Public pages allow OpenAI’s search crawler.

Refine the interface during the project

As KorisLab grew, the first interface felt too much like a generic AI-generated dashboard. Competing card colours and highlights made the analysis harder to read. I refined the identity and hierarchy so the product’s depth was easier to see.

Green carries the brand and orange guides data comparisons. Headline figures, units and supporting context have clearer roles. Recent-game values, season baselines and shared chart axes help make comparisons readable.

I also connected the home page more closely to the product. Player imagery and dither give it character, while the dashboard preview uses the same working overview component as the application, with hover details and links into data views.

These captures document a local iteration awaiting production migration. Usability and discovery gains have not yet been measured.

A first design system for working with an agent

I created the first version of KorisLab’s design system to make collaboration with a coding agent easier. Design tokens and reusable components exist in Paper, with corresponding tokens and components in the application code.

Through Paper MCP, the agent can inspect my designs and use them as a reference for implementation. I direct the design, guide the implementation and review the interface in the browser. The system gives us a shared foundation to extend as the product develops.

Extend the data before extending the claims

The current product mainly uses Basket.fi statistics and distinguishes verified figures from calculated estimates. Validation remains ongoing, and not every game is available to every tool.

I have emailed the Finnish Basketball Association to ask about further data availability and am awaiting a response. Longer term, I want to explore computer vision for more detailed game data. That is a future direction, not part of the current analytics.
